## Summary

The article argues, drawing on five decades of research, that:

1. **Individual developer variance (10x–25x) dwarfs any process effect** — Sackman (1968), confirmed by Curtis, Mills, DeMarco & Lister, Boehm, and others.
2. **Developer skill follows a Pareto distribution** (Price's Law), making "uniformly competent" teams extraordinarily rare.
3. **Environment and people are the process** — Peopleware's Coding War Games showed organizational clustering, not methodology, predicts performance.
4. **Group capability predicts outcomes** — Garett Jones's *Hive Mind* research shows average group capability matters more than rules.
5. **Scrum's own effectiveness research proves the point** — every factor Verwijs identified maps to people quality, not process quality.
6. **The Standish Group data agrees** — team skill level is the strongest predictor, stronger than Agile vs. Waterfall.
7. **The industry prefers process over talent for structural reasons** — process provides camouflage for the people who would be exposed by a talent-first approach.
